What are your rights?

About the right of children and young people to stay safe, and the duty of SOS Children’s Villages.

Introduction

Let’s start with your rights. Every child in the world has special rights, and they are set out in the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child. It is like a big promise from many countries that they will make sure children’s rights are protected.

All of these rights are important, and no one can take them away from children. This means you have the right to be safe and protected from anything that could hurt you, such as abuse, neglect or exploitation (being taken advantage of). SOS Children’s Villages is committed to upholding these rights for children and young people.

General information

SOS Children’s Villages is committed to protecting children’s rights and creating a safe and supportive place where children and young people can build strong, trusting relationships and be the best they can be. SOS Children’s Villages sets out everything it promises to do to keep children and young people safe in a document called the Child and Youth Safeguarding Policy.

Child and youth safeguarding means that SOS Children’s Villages takes every possible step to make sure its staff, everyone who works with it and all its programmes do nothing that might hurt children and young people or put them at risk. If something does go wrong, SOS Children’s Villages promises to deal with it in the right way. Harm can come from things like abuse, neglect or exploitation, and SOS Children’s Villages is determined to prevent it.

Youth Participation in Safeguarding

Children and young people have the right to be part of everything that affects them, especially their safety and decisions about their lives. It is important for them to know their rights, understand how SOS Children’s Villages keeps them safe, and know how to report a concern and get help if they need it.

SOS Children’s Villages believes that children and young people are the real experts in their own lives, so it wants to hear what you have to say about what might be risky or how things can be made better. When children and young people help to create a safe environment, SOS Children’s Villages can better understand their needs and make sure its programmes work well. This way, everyone can have a safe and supportive place to grow up.
