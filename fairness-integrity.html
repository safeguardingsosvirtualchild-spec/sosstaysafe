Fairness & Integrity

Play Fair. Stay Safe.

Spot unfairness. Use your voice. Be the change.

This page helps you understand what corruption and unfairness can look like in everyday life, how they affect young people and communities, and what you can do when something does not feel right. You will also learn how the SOS values of Courage, Commitment, Trust and Accountability can help you make fair choices and speak up safely.

Try the Power Check Game What can I do?

The Ripple of Unfairness

Corruption isn’t just about big, far-away scandals. It is the small, unfair choices that ripple out and hurt people’s fair access to opportunities – chances to get healthcare, go to a good school or feel safe.

When someone abuses the power entrusted to them for their own gain – even a little – children, young people and families are often the ones who feel it most.

Select a place to see the ripple.

School An unfair choice here means fewer textbooks.

Hospital One bribe here means less medicine for a family.

Home and community Unfairness here leaves a family without clean water.

One unfair act. Many real victims.

What Is Corruption?

At SOS Children’s Villages, corruption means the abuse of entrusted power for private gain.

It doesn’t only happen at the top – everyone has some level of entrusted power and responsibility. Learning to recognise corruption is the first step; knowing how to respond safely is the next.

Power Check Game

Read each scenario and decide: is this Fairness or Corruption?

Low power situation

The school captain is trusted to make sure students keep the school clean. One day, she sees someone littering. Instead of reporting it, she tells the student: “If you do my chores for a week, I won’t tell anyone.”

Corruption: Favouritism and abuse of a position for personal gain.

High power situation

A local leader controls access to clean water. He tells families they must pay him money before he opens the tap for them.

Corruption: Forcing people to pay unfairly for something they have a right to is extortion.

A caregiver explains the house rules clearly to all young people and applies those same rules equally to everyone, including personal favourites.

Fair behaviour

Fairness: Fairness means applying rules equally, regardless of personal preference.

Make the Fair Choice: An Integrity Challenge

Work through seven short, real-life situations about integrity, abuse of power, corruption and safe reporting. After each choice, you will see why it matters.

Your class is collecting money for a school trip. The student treasurer buys herself lunch with some of it and says she will pay it back later. Is this corruption?

Integrity principle: Using entrusted funds

Using entrusted funds for personal benefit, even for a short time, is a misuse of trust. The amount does not change the principle.

A sports coach always selects players whose parents give him gifts, rather than the best players. What is happening?

Integrity principle: Abuse of position

Using a position to get personal benefits at the expense of others is an abuse of power.

You see a staff member take community equipment home. What is the best first step?

Integrity principle: Safe reporting

Safe reporting channels help make sure concerns are handled properly, and they protect young people from having to take matters into their own hands.

A young leader shares out resources equally to all members, even though some of them are her close friends. What does this show?

Integrity principle: Fair treatment

Integrity means making fair decisions, even when personal relationships could influence you.

The community bicycles

At a community resource centre, you see the coordinator loading donated bicycles into his personal car. He says they are faulty and need repair, but they look new. What should you do?

You do not need to investigate or prove wrongdoing yourself. Share what you observed through the appropriate channel.

Report a concern on SpeakUp (opens in a new tab)↗

Now see the other path

What happens when you speak up?

You report exactly what you saw to a trusted youth leader. The concern is reviewed through the appropriate process, and the bicycles are eventually made available to the young people.

Why it matters

Speaking up through a safe channel can help protect community resources and make sure everyone is treated fairly.

Now see the other path

What happens when we stay silent?

You decide not to say anything because you are unsure whether the situation is wrong. Later, the bicycles are no longer available to the community.

Why it matters

Silence can allow concerns to stay unresolved. You do not have to confront anyone directly. When something does not feel right, ask for advice or use a safe reporting channel.

Challenge complete!

Every fair choice counts. Use what you are trusted with honestly, treat people fairly and, when something does not feel right, speak up safely.

How to Start Today

You don’t need to wait until you’re older. Here’s how you can start your journey today.

  1. 1

    Learn more

    Read the Pathways to Integrity Manual by SOS Children’s Villages. Also explore the UNODC “Stolen Futures” report, which shows how corruption affects young people’s futures.

    UNODC: Stolen Futures (PDF) (opens in a new tab)↗
  2. 2

    Talk about it

    Help your friends and classmates learn what corruption looks like and why it hurts everyone. Use the school captain example.

  3. 3

    Pay attention

    Notice situations – big or small – where something feels unfair or dishonest. Ask yourself:

    Does this feel right? Would I be okay if everyone knew?

  4. 4

    Report safely

    If you see suspected corruption, use the safe SpeakUp channel. It accepts both safeguarding and anti-corruption reports.

    Report a concern on SpeakUp (opens in a new tab)↗
  5. 5

    Be a role model

    Show integrity in your daily actions by being honest, keeping your promises and treating others fairly.

    Next small choice – choose the fair path.

Your Voice = Power

No matter how old you are or where you live, you have the power to build a community where honesty and respect win. By learning about corruption and choosing integrity, you are already part of the solution.
